# 1. Executive definition

Husky Tech Repairs should be understood as a technology repair and
device-lifecycle company, not a kiosk with a larger website.

Husky repairs consumer electronics, performs deeper board-level
diagnostics and microsoldering, sells transparent refurbished devices,
provides supported privacy-focused device services, recovers value from
old hardware, and serves individuals, businesses, schools and trade
partners through a lean operating model.

The website is not merely a marketing surface. Over time it should
become the customer-facing operating interface for the business:
diagnosis intake, booking, logistics, case tracking, refurbished
inventory, configuration, knowledge publishing, business account
workflows and eventually lifecycle history.

Husky should feel capable without pretending to be large. Its authority
should come from clarity, visible process, technical proof and unusually
transparent product design --- not inflated adjectives.

# 2. The market problem Husky is solving

The repair category is crowded, but much of the communication is
interchangeable.

Most repair businesses sell the same surface-level claims: fast service,
cheap prices, quality parts, expert technicians, same-day repairs and
"all devices repaired." Because every competitor says similar things,
the customer is left comparing proximity, price and reviews.

Husky should not try to win by shouting those claims louder. It should
change what the customer is comparing. The brand should make the
customer care about diagnosis quality, repair depth, parts transparency,
lifecycle value, privacy choices and what actually happens to a device
after it leaves their hands.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  "Quality phone repairs"             Show how diagnosis works and when
                                      part replacement is not enough.

                                      original, reclaimed, aftermarket or
                                      replaced.

  "We repair everything"              Invite unusual devices while
                                      refusing false guarantees: "If it
                                      has a motherboard, ask us."

                                      reuse, refurbish, harvest, recycle.

# 4. Communications doctrine

Husky should use a Lulu Cheng-inspired communications approach: make a
non-obvious idea legible, build a memorable worldview, go direct to the
people who matter, and use proof instead of institutional-sounding
claims.

This does not mean copying another person's phrasing or turning every
page into a manifesto. It means the company should be able to explain
why it exists in language a smart non-specialist can understand, then
back that explanation with product behavior.

The website should repeatedly translate technical differences into
customer consequences. Technical terms are useful when they identify a
real mechanism; they are harmful when they exist only to make Husky
sound sophisticated.

## 4.1 The communications rules

> **1.** Lead with the customer's reality, not the company biography.
>
> **2.** Explain the old assumption before presenting Husky's
> alternative.
>
> **3.** Turn technical differences into concrete consequences.
>
> **4.** Use simple words until a precise technical word is genuinely
> more useful.
>
> **5.** Prefer demonstrations, records, case studies and process
> diagrams over adjectives.
>
> **6.** Go direct: publish useful knowledge rather than relying on
> third parties to explain Husky.
>
> **7.** Create memorable phrases sparingly. A few repeated ideas beat
> dozens of slogans.
>
> **8.** Never imply certainty where diagnostics are required.
>
> **9.** Never manufacture a movement around trivial features. The
> belief must connect to actual product behavior.
>
> **10.** Write so the same page is useful to a customer, a search
> engine, an AI retrieval system and a future employee.

## 4.2 The message pattern

# 7. Repair

The repair experience should begin with the problem, not a wall of
device icons.

Husky repairs common devices such as iPhones, Samsung phones, tablets,
laptops and consoles, but the experience should remain open to less
common electronics. The site should make common repairs easy to navigate
while preserving an "other / unusual device" path.

The brand should not say that anything with a motherboard is guaranteed
repairable. The phrase is an invitation to diagnose, not a promise that
every device is technically or economically recoverable.

## 7.1 Common repair categories

-   Displays and glass

-   Batteries

-   Charging and ports

-   Cameras and sensors

-   Buttons and housings

-   Speakers and microphones

-   Software and operating-system issues

-   Liquid exposure

-   No-power / intermittent faults

-   Storage and data-related cases

-   Console faults

# 8. Husky Board Lab

Board Lab is the technical credibility engine of the brand. It should
make complex repair understandable rather than turning the site into an
electronics textbook.

Board Lab exists for faults that cannot be solved by replacing the
obvious assembly. It covers deeper diagnosis, board-level repair and
microsoldering where technically feasible and economically sensible.

This capability also lets Husky serve a second market: repair shops and
IT providers that can perform routine work but need somewhere to
escalate difficult faults.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *"Most repair shops replace parts. Sometimes the part isn't the
  problem."*
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *"When replacing parts stops working, diagnosis begins."*
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## 8.1 Typical Board Lab cases

-   No power

-   Charging faults that persist after port replacement

-   Liquid and corrosion damage

-   Short circuits

-   Damaged connectors or board pads

-   Power-rail faults

-   Display/data-line faults

-   Board damage after prior repair

-   Intermittent failures

-   Selected data-recovery scenarios

-   Unusual electronics where board access and documentation permit
    diagnosis

## 8.2 Board Lab conversion path

> **1.** Customer describes symptoms and prior repair attempts.
>
> **2.** Husky determines whether the job belongs in standard repair or
> Board Lab.
>
> **3.** Device is received or collected.
>
> **4.** Initial inspection/diagnosis is performed under the business's
> defined fee/quote policy.
>
> **5.** Customer receives a concise explanation and recommendation.
>
> **6.** If approved, repair proceeds and is then tested.
>
> **7.** Case history is retained for customer support and future
> knowledge content where consent and privacy rules allow.

# 9. Refurbished and "Build Your Refurb"

Husky should turn a weakness of the refurbishment market --- hidden
component quality --- into the product itself.

Two refurbished phones with the same model name can contain very
different displays, batteries, housings and histories. Most stores
collapse that complexity into one vague grade. Husky should expose the
choices that materially affect price and experience.

The configurator should be based on real inventory and real supported
combinations. It must never imply that every component can be freely
mixed when supply, device architecture or service policy makes that
impossible.

# 10. Privacy and GrapheneOS services

Privacy is a practical service line, not a fear-based marketing theme.

Husky may install GrapheneOS on supported devices, sell compatible
devices configured with GrapheneOS, and offer clearly defined optional
hardware modifications such as camera or microphone removal where
technically feasible and legally appropriate.

The page should explain what each choice changes, what it does not
change, and what functionality may be lost. Husky must not imply
anonymity, untraceability, immunity from law enforcement,
"government-proof" operation or other absolute security claims.

Device compatibility and support status must be checked against current
official GrapheneOS guidance at the time of sale or service. The website
data model should allow supported devices to change without rewriting
the site.

# 11. Recycling and circularity

Recycling should be the last stage of a hierarchy, not a green label
placed on disposal.

Husky's circular model begins by asking whether a device can continue to
provide value. The preferred sequence is reuse, repair, refurbishment,
component recovery and finally responsible recycling for material that
cannot sensibly stay in service.

This is more credible than generic "eco" messaging because customers can
see the decision logic.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Priority**                        **Outcome**
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  1\. Reuse                           Device remains in service with
                                      minimal intervention.

  2\. Repair                          Fault is corrected and the device
                                      returns to use.

  3\. Refurbish                       Device is rebuilt, graded and
                                      returned to market.

  4\. Harvest                         Usable components support other
                                      repairs/refurbishments where
                                      appropriate.

  5\. Recycle                         Remaining material is sent through
                                      an appropriate recycling pathway.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  *"The most sustainable device is often the one that already exists."*
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Trust architecture

Trust should be designed into the product rather than added later as
badges.

A repair customer gives Husky expensive personal hardware, often
containing sensitive data. A refurbished buyer cannot directly inspect
every component. A business customer needs operational predictability.
These are trust-heavy transactions.

Husky should therefore turn uncertainty into visible process wherever
possible.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Trust problem**                   **Product response**
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  "What are you doing to my device?"  Case status and plain-language work
                                      notes.

  "What part am I paying for?"        Parts categories and definitions.

  "Is this refurb actually good?"     Build sheet, condition rules and
                                      component provenance.

  "Will you lose my data?"            Clear data handling explanation and
                                      backup guidance.

  "Can you actually do board work?"   Real diagnosis cases, real
                                      photography, specific capability
                                      pages.

  "What happens if it cannot be       Explicit unrepairable/uneconomical
  repaired?"                          outcome process.

  "Are these privacy claims real?"    Compatibility, trade-offs and
                                      non-absolute claims.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

## 15.1 Evidence hierarchy

> **1.** Real process
>
> **2.** Real completed cases
>
> **3.** Real customer reviews where permission/platform terms allow
>
> **4.** Real first-party data
>

# 21. Knowledge, SEO and GEO system

The goal is not to produce the largest quantity of repair pages. The
goal is to become one of the clearest and most useful primary sources in
the category.

Husky should be optimized for conventional search and generative
retrieval by building a clean entity structure, answering real
questions, publishing original evidence, using consistent terminology
and making important information available as text rather than hiding it
only in graphics or client-side interactions.

The site must not create hundreds of thin suburb pages, fake FAQs,
mass-generated articles or duplicated device pages merely to capture
keywords. That destroys brand quality and creates long-term search risk.

## 21.1 GEO principles

> **1.** Answer the question directly before expanding.
>
> **2.** Name entities consistently: device, model, repair, part type,
> symptom, service method.
>
> **3.** Publish real first-party repair cases and aggregate insights.
>
> **4.** Maintain dates and compatibility status on technical/privacy
> content.
>
> **5.** Use descriptive headings that match real customer questions.
>
> **6.** Connect claims to evidence and explain uncertainty.
>
> **7.** Build strong internal links between device, problem, service
> and knowledge pages.
>
> **8.** Make local/service-area facts explicit and machine-readable.
>
> **9.** Avoid exaggerated superlatives that retrieval systems cannot
> substantiate.
>
> **10.** Write content worth citing independently of whether an AI
> system ever cites it.

## 21.2 First-party data opportunities

-   Repairability outcomes by device/model

-   Most common failure modes

-   Repair-versus-replace economics by age bracket

-   Battery health distributions in refurbished inventory

-   Board Lab success/diagnostic outcome categories

-   Average component reuse in refurbishment

-   Recycling outcomes by device category

-   Business fleet failure patterns

Any published data must be anonymised, statistically honest and based on
a meaningful sample. Small samples should be labelled rather than made
to look authoritative.

# 25. Technical platform principles

The implementation should be modern but intentionally boring where
reliability matters.

-   Fast server-rendered or statically generated public pages where
    appropriate.

-   Progressive enhancement: important content remains accessible
    without heavy client-side code.

-   Component system shared across public pages, booking, builder and
    account surfaces.

-   Strong form validation and resumable multi-step flows.

-   Authentication only where it creates value; do not force an account
    for simple enquiries unless necessary.

-   Role-based access for staff tooling.

-   Audit trail for repair case status and customer approvals.

-   Image optimisation for high-resolution repair photography.

-   Accessibility target at least WCAG 2.2 AA in implementation
    practice.

-   Performance budgets for JavaScript, images and animation.

-   Consent-aware analytics.

-   Automated tests around booking, inventory availability,
    checkout/configuration and status changes.

-   Backups and clear recovery procedures for operational data.

## 25.1 Suggested implementation posture

A framework such as Next.js can support the public site and
application-like flows, but the final technology choice matters less
than the architecture: typed schemas, real content models, minimal
duplication, strong performance and an admin experience Luke can
actually use.

# 29. Things Husky must never do

These are brand protections, not stylistic preferences.

-   Never claim every device is repairable.

-   Never imply GrapheneOS or hardware modifications make a user
    anonymous or untraceable.

-   Never hide aftermarket parts behind ambiguous language.

-   Never fabricate reviews, repair statistics, locations, turnaround
    times or certifications.

-   Never create hundreds of low-value suburb pages solely for SEO.

-   Never turn every technical idea into a branded proprietary term.

-   Never use fear to sell privacy.

-   Never use "eco" claims without explaining the actual lifecycle
    practice.

-   Never make the customer decode electronics jargon to understand
    their choices.

-   Never let visual spectacle slow down booking or obscure essential
    information.

-   Never make "AI" part of the proposition unless it creates an
    observable customer benefit.

-   Never let an autonomous content agent publish factual claims without
    a verification path.

-   Never promise same-day or guaranteed turnaround as a default brand
    claim unless the business can support it consistently.

# Appendix E --- The Husky brand manifesto

This is internal alignment copy, not necessarily homepage copy.

Useful technology should not become disposable because the obvious
repair failed.

A customer should not need to be an electronics technician to understand
what happened to their device.

A refurbished phone should not be a black box. If a screen, battery or
housing changed, the buyer should be able to know what they are paying
for.

Privacy should be explained as choices and trade-offs, not sold through
fear.

Recycling should begin with the question: can this still be useful?

A small repair business can operate with the clarity and systems of a
much larger company without pretending to be one.

Husky earns trust by showing the work: the diagnosis, the parts, the
process, the limits and the outcome.

The brand does not need to say it is innovative. The customer should
notice that the experience is different.

The long-term goal is simple: keep good technology alive, make repair
easier to understand, and give every device a more intelligent path
through its useful life.
