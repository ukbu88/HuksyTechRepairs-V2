CANONICAL CODEX + BAND EXECUTION SPECIFICATION

Husky Build Command

A production execution brief for building Husky Tech Repairs as a
modular, accessible, conversion-oriented Next.js system on Vercel ---
with a four-agent Band room and browser-based verification.

North star: make complicated repair capability feel simple, credible and
useful --- then make every next action obvious.

**PRIMARY EXECUTOR:** Codex

**AGENT ROOM:** Opus 5.5 Director · 2× Luna High Integrators · Sonnet
High Verifier

**TARGET STACK:** Professional Next.js App Router · strict TypeScript ·
Vercel

**COMPANION SOURCE:** Husky Tech Repairs Brand + Product + Digital Canon

**BUILD CONSTRAINT:** No invented business facts. No generic AI SaaS
aesthetic. Modular feature control from day one.

# 0. EXECUTIVE COMMAND

Codex: prepare a fresh Band room and use it to build Husky Tech Repairs
from the supplied Husky Brand + Product + Digital Canon.

The build must be capable of launching as a narrow business today and
expanding without re-platforming later. The site may launch with only
Board Lab enabled, only consumer repairs enabled, or with the full Husky
service architecture. Whole divisions must be switchable without
deleting code, leaving dead navigation, leaking disabled routes into
search, or creating a broken homepage.

This is not a prototype, hackathon demo, generic template, or
AI-generated marketing page. Treat it as a real production website for a
real Brisbane repair business. The standard is professional engineering,
unusually clear communication, high trust, strong conversion design,
visual identity with restraint, excellent accessibility, excellent
mobile behaviour, and a codebase another senior developer can understand
without asking the original agents what they meant.

The site must feel more considered than the typical repair-shop site
while remaining understandable to an ordinary customer who does not care
about technology. Technical depth is evidence. It is not the headline.

The brand doctrine is founder-led and direct: explain the problem, state
Husky\'s belief, prove the capability, then provide the next action.
Reduce uncertainty before asking for commitment. Do not use jargon as
status signalling. Do not imitate SaaS landing-page conventions just
because they are easy to generate.

The supplied Husky Canon is the product and brand authority. This
document is the execution authority. If an implementation choice
conflicts with the Canon, stop and surface the conflict to the Director
agent. Do not silently reinterpret the brand.

# 2. PRODUCT POSITIONING THAT MUST SURVIVE IMPLEMENTATION

Husky Tech Repairs is not to be represented as a generic phone-repair
kiosk.

The brand territory is:

-   repair before replacement;

-   deeper diagnostics when simple part replacement is not enough;

-   transparent refurbishment;

-   understandable privacy services;

-   practical mail-in and pickup logistics;

-   business and school repair workflows;

-   device reuse, parts recovery and responsible recycling;

-   public technical knowledge that proves competence without
    overwhelming the customer.

Core brand ideas from the Canon include:

-   **Keep good technology alive.**

-   **If it has a motherboard, ask us.**

-   **Know exactly what\'s inside your refurbished phone.**

-   **When replacing the obvious part doesn\'t solve the fault, diagnose
    deeper.**

-   **Broken devices shouldn\'t become IT projects.**

-   **Privacy you can understand.**

These are message territories, not excuses to repeat slogans on every
page.

The required communication pattern is:

**Problem -\> belief -\> proof -\> action.**

Example:

-   Problem: two refurbished phones can look identical while containing
    very different replacement parts.

-   Belief: buyers should know what they are paying for.

-   Proof: Husky exposes part provenance and lets customers choose
    available build options.

-   Action: build a refurbished device.

Every major page should be able to explain itself to a smart teenager,
an older customer, a school administrator and a technically
sophisticated repair customer without becoming four different brands.

# 3. HARD ANTI-SLOP RULES

The following are failure conditions.

## 3.1 Visual AI slop

Do not create the standard generated SaaS composition:

-   huge gradient blob behind a centred hero;

-   endless floating rounded cards;

-   glassmorphism everywhere;

-   six identical feature cards with Lucide icons;

-   pointless dashboard screenshots for a non-dashboard product;

-   giant pill buttons everywhere;

-   neon purple/blue gradients because no visual decision was made;

-   animated text merely because a motion library is installed;

-   fake trust logos;

-   fake metrics;

-   generic stock photos of smiling people holding phones;

-   excessive border-radius that makes every section look like the same
    component;

-   alternating light/dark bands without content reason;

-   decorative grids, noise and glow effects used as substitutes for art
    direction.

The design should be industrial, editorial, product-focused and
physical. Real repair photography, boards, components, devices, tools
and process are stronger than abstract decoration.

## 3.2 Copy slop

Avoid unproved phrases such as:

-   world-class;

-   cutting-edge;

-   seamless;

-   premium quality;

-   industry-leading;

-   revolutionary;

-   trusted experts;

-   state-of-the-art;

-   best in Brisbane;

-   unmatched service;

-   secure/private/genuine without explaining the claim.

Prefer concrete language.

Do not describe Husky using ten technical nouns when one ordinary
sentence is enough.

Technical terms may appear where they explain an actual service, fault
or proof point. They should not be used to make the brand sound clever.

## 3.3 Engineering slop

Do not:

-   use any to make TypeScript errors disappear;

-   duplicate route/page data across components;

-   hard-code feature visibility separately in navigation, homepage and
    sitemap;

-   scatter environment variable reads through the component tree;

-   put every component behind \"use client\";

-   fetch data client-side when the server can render it;

-   install a large dependency for a trivial helper;

-   ship unused motion libraries;

-   use one 1,000-line page component;

-   hide errors with empty catch blocks;

-   create mock APIs that appear real in production;

-   store secrets in client bundles;

-   commit credentials;

-   create a CMS dependency merely to say a CMS exists;

-   use disabled links as placeholders on production navigation;

-   expose disabled service pages in sitemaps or structured data.

## Agent D --- VERIFIER

**Model:** Sonnet High

**Role:** Browser QA / Adversarial Verification Engineer

**Authority:** Can block acceptance. Does not own product design.

This agent must be given tools that let it view the actual website, not
only source code.

Required capabilities where Band supports them:

-   browser navigation;

-   rendered page screenshots;

-   Playwright or equivalent browser automation;

-   browser console inspection;

-   network-error inspection;

-   viewport switching;

-   keyboard navigation;

-   reduced-motion emulation;

-   accessibility inspection;

-   DOM inspection only as a supplement to visual review.

Install/use Playwright in the repository even if Band itself provides a
browser. The project must retain repeatable browser tests after the
agent room is gone.

Recommended accessibility integration: \@axe-core/playwright or an
equivalent current, maintained axe integration.

### Verifier operating posture

The Verifier is adversarial.

It asks:

-   Does this look generated?

-   Can I understand what Husky does within seconds?

-   Is the primary next action obvious?

-   Does the page make an unproved claim?

-   Does mobile feel designed or merely collapsed?

-   Can an older user read and use it comfortably?

-   Does a keyboard user get trapped?

-   Do feature flags actually remove the disabled division everywhere?

-   Does Board-Lab-only mode still look intentional?

-   Are there dead routes?

-   Does content overflow at 200% zoom?

-   Are motion and hover states required to understand anything?

-   Is there a button that looks clickable but is not?

-   Does the form clearly explain what happens next?

-   Is JavaScript doing work that HTML/server rendering should do?

-   Are there hydration warnings, console errors or layout shifts?

The Verifier may propose fixes, test patches and small QA-only changes.
It must not silently make broad brand decisions. Product/design
disagreements go to Director.

### Verifier outputs

Maintain:

-   ops/band/VERIFY.md

-   ops/band/ISSUES.md

Each issue includes:

-   severity: blocker / major / minor;

-   route;

-   viewport;

-   reproduction steps;

-   screenshot path where applicable;

-   expected behaviour;

-   actual behaviour;

-   suggested direction, not forced implementation.

# 5. COMMUNICATION AND AUTHORITY MODEL

The room must not become four agents editing the same files at once.

Use an explicit pipeline:

**Director -\> Integrators -\> Integration build -\> Verifier -\>
Director acceptance**

Rules:

> **1.** Director defines a task and acceptance criteria before code
> begins.
>
> **2.** Each task has exactly one implementation owner.
>
> **3.** Integrators may request an interface from the other integrator,
> but should not casually edit the other\'s domain.
>
> **4.** Shared contracts are approved by Director first.
>
> **5.** Verifier reviews the integrated result, not isolated
> screenshots from an agent\'s local idea.
>
> **6.** A Verifier blocker returns the work to the task owner.
>
> **7.** Director closes the issue only after re-verification.
>
> **8.** Agents write short handoff summaries after each task.
>
> **9.** No agent relies on chat memory as the only record of an
> architectural decision.
>
> **10.** Canonical state lives in repository documents and code.

If Band supports branches/worktrees, prefer:

-   band/director

-   band/experience

-   band/systems

-   band/verify

Use worktrees or equivalent isolation so agents do not overwrite one
another.

The Director controls merge order.

# 7. REQUIRED TECHNICAL BASELINE

Use the latest stable compatible toolchain available at execution time.
Do not pin versions merely because they appear in this document.

Required architectural posture:

-   Next.js App Router;

-   React Server Components by default;

-   TypeScript strict mode;

-   Vercel as production host;

-   Node.js runtime for normal server work unless a specific feature has
    a documented reason to require another runtime;

-   semantic HTML;

-   server rendering/static generation where appropriate;

-   progressive enhancement for core informational content;

-   client components only for actual client interactivity;

-   image optimisation through Next.js where appropriate;

-   modern font loading via next/font or equivalent local/optimised
    approach;

-   package manager chosen once and locked;

-   reproducible scripts for lint, typecheck, test, e2e and build.

Recommended tooling, subject to current compatibility:

-   Tailwind CSS as a styling implementation tool if useful, but never
    as an aesthetic template;

-   CSS custom properties for design tokens;

-   Zod for shared validation/config schemas;

-   Playwright for E2E and visual/smoke verification;

-   axe integration for automated accessibility checks;

-   Vitest or the current project-standard equivalent for focused unit
    tests;

-   GSAP only for signature motion that cannot be expressed cleanly with
    CSS;

-   MDX or a typed local-content system for the first Knowledge
    implementation;

-   provider adapters rather than vendor calls scattered through UI
    code.

Do not add a UI kit that visually dictates the product. If a headless
primitive library is needed for accessibility, style it completely to
Husky\'s system.

## 8.2 Vercel-native flags

Prefer Vercel Flags for production feature control when available for
the project. Vercel Flags reached general availability in 2026 and
provides a Next.js-compatible Flags SDK. Build an adapter so the
application does not become inseparable from one provider.

Conceptual layering:

FeatureDefinition\
↓\
FeatureProvider interface\
↓\
Vercel Flags adapter (production)\
↓\
Environment/static adapter (local/test/fallback)

Do not read process.env directly inside arbitrary page components.

## 8.3 Feature behaviour must cascade

A disabled feature must affect:

-   main navigation;

-   utility navigation;

-   homepage service blocks;

-   contextual CTAs;

-   route access;

-   generated sitemap;

-   structured data;

-   internal link suggestions;

-   search/index pages;

-   footer links;

-   related-content modules;

-   booking options;

-   business-service lists where relevant.

No disabled division should remain discoverable through a stray link.

## 8.4 Route policy

Director chooses a consistent policy per feature type:

-   notFound() for truly unavailable public routes; or

-   redirect to the closest enabled parent when that is better UX.

Do not render a half-empty page saying \"coming soon\" unless Luke
explicitly wants a public waitlist/announcement.

## 8.5 Launch profiles

On top of individual flags, define optional launch profiles as
convenience presets.

Suggested profiles:

-   board-lab-only

-   repair-core

-   repair-plus-business

-   full-minus-refurb

-   full

Profiles seed defaults; individual flags remain the source of truth.

## 8.6 Adaptive homepage

The homepage must respond intelligently to enabled capabilities.

Example:

If only boardLab is enabled, the homepage must not look like a mutilated
full-service homepage. It should become a focused Board Lab proposition
with appropriate proof, process and enquiry CTA.

If repair, boardLab, business and mailIn are enabled, the homepage
should present that coherent service set.

Do this via typed composition/data rules, not dozens of JSX ternaries.

# 10. DESIGN SYSTEM

The site should feel like a precise modern technology company with a
physical workshop behind it.

Target character:

**industrial + editorial + product-focused + human**

Not:

**cyberpunk + gamer + repair kiosk + generic SaaS +
luxury-for-luxury\'s-sake**

## 10.1 Colour

Start from a predominantly white/light system unless Canon changes.

Use dark sections intentionally for contrast or technical depth, not as
the default personality.

Choose one restrained accent with a real role in hierarchy/status. Do
not spray the accent across every component.

Do not let the palette reduce readability.

## 10.2 Typography

Typography is one of the primary identity tools.

Requirements:

-   highly legible body face;

-   distinct but restrained display treatment;

-   no novelty typeface that damages older-user readability;

-   robust numeric/technical rendering for repair/build sheets;

-   body copy should be comfortable, not tiny;

-   line length controlled;

-   clear hierarchy without making every heading enormous.

Suggested baseline:

-   mobile body: no smaller than 16px for normal content;

-   key explanatory copy may sit at 17-19px;

-   comfortable line height;

-   avoid ultra-light font weights.

The Experience Integrator may select an open/licensable font pair.
Record the decision.

## 10.3 Spacing and shape

Use whitespace to create confidence.

Do not place every piece of content inside a card.

Use borders, rules, grids, labels and layout like an
editorial/industrial system.

Corner radii should be intentional and restrained.

## 10.4 Images

Prefer:

-   real repair benches;

-   microscopes;

-   board macro photography;

-   devices in process;

-   hands doing real work;

-   component details;

-   packaging/mail-in process;

-   before/after evidence where truthful.

Create clearly marked placeholders if real photography is not yet
available. Do not ship fake documentary photography presented as Luke\'s
actual work.

## 10.5 Signature interactions

Potential signature interactions from the Canon:

-   an interactive but comprehensible Board Lab graphic;

-   Refurb builder that updates a build sheet;

-   repair intake that asks one clear question at a time;

-   lifecycle graphic: reuse -\> repair/refurbish -\> harvest -\>
    recycle;

-   subtle exploded-device/component view if performance allows.

Pick only interactions that make the brand more memorable or the service
easier to understand.

# 11. ACCESSIBILITY AND AGE-INCLUSIVE UX

Target WCAG 2.2 AA in implementation practice.

The site must be comfortable for older customers as well as young
technical users.

Minimum expectations:

-   semantic landmarks;

-   visible keyboard focus;

-   skip link;

-   full keyboard operation;

-   sensible tab order;

-   accessible labels and error messages;

-   sufficient colour contrast;

-   no meaning conveyed by colour alone;

-   no tiny low-contrast secondary text;

-   touch targets preferably around 44px even when the formal minimum
    could be smaller;

-   forms that explain errors next to the relevant field and in an
    accessible summary where useful;

-   no placeholder-only labels;

-   no hover-only essential information;

-   no motion required to understand content;

-   prefers-reduced-motion respected;

-   headings in logical order;

-   meaningful link text;

-   aria used only when semantic HTML cannot express the interaction;

-   content works at 200% browser zoom without loss or overlap;

-   mobile layout remains usable at narrow widths;

-   phone numbers/contact methods rendered in practical forms;

-   plain-English explanation before technical detail.

The Verifier must test at least one full booking/enquiry journey using
only the keyboard.

# 12. PERFORMANCE STANDARD

This site should feel immediate on ordinary phones, not only on a
development MacBook.

Engineering principles:

-   Server Components by default.

-   Avoid shipping client JavaScript for static explanatory sections.

-   Lazy-load heavy interactive modules.

-   Code-split motion/3D/visualisation dependencies.

-   Optimise images and set dimensions to avoid layout shift.

-   Do not autoplay heavyweight video on mobile by default.

-   Prefer native browser capabilities where appropriate.

-   Avoid large icon libraries when a tiny subset is enough.

-   Audit bundle impact before adding libraries.

-   Do not use WebGL for decoration merely because it looks technical.

-   Keep critical path typography and CSS controlled.

Performance budgets should be defined by the Systems Integrator and
approved by Director.

Use Lighthouse/Web Vitals as signals, not as a substitute for real
interaction testing.

Create typed schemas for at least:

-   feature definitions;

-   services;

-   device families;

-   repair types;

-   logistics options;

-   business audiences;

-   knowledge articles;

-   FAQ items;

-   calls to action;

-   refurbishment configuration dimensions;

-   future inventory records;

-   booking/enquiry data.

Suggested conceptual service shape:

interface ServiceDefinition {\

## 13.1 Content source

For the first production pass, prefer a local, typed content system over
introducing a heavyweight CMS merely to meet a checklist.

Knowledge may use MDX/Markdown with typed frontmatter and build-time
validation.

Service content may use typed TypeScript/JSON/YAML depending on
ergonomics.

Abstract the access layer enough that a CMS can be introduced later
without rewriting page components.

Do not make content editing so abstract that Luke cannot understand it.

# 14. BOOKING / ENQUIRY FLOW

The first version should optimise for **qualified enquiry**, not pretend
Husky has a sophisticated operations platform before it exists.

Flow concept:

> **1.** What needs help?
>
> **2.** What device is it?
>
> **3.** What is happening?
>
> **4.** How would you like to get it to Husky? (only enabled logistics
> options)
>
> **5.** Contact details.
>
> **6.** Optional notes/photos where supported.
>
> **7.** Confirmation explaining exactly what happens next.

Allow \"I don\'t know\" / \"Something else\" paths.

Do not force account creation.

Do not ask for unnecessary personal information.

Use Zod or equivalent validation on both client/server boundaries as
appropriate.

## 21.1 Lulu-style communications principles to operationalise

Use these as communication mechanics, not imitation of a person\'s exact
wording:

> **1. Go direct.** Say what the company believes rather than hiding
> behind neutral marketing filler.
>
> **2. Translate complexity.** Explain difficult technology in language
> a smart non-expert can repeat to another person.
>
> **3. Create a point of view.** Husky should stand for repair,
> transparency and understandable technical choices.
>
> **4. Lead with the promised outcome.** The customer cares about
> preserving their device/data/time before they care about component
> names.
>
> **5. Use proof instead of adjectives.** Show the board, the part
> provenance, the test, the case or the process.
>
> **6. Make the difference legible.** If Husky does something
> differently, the page should demonstrate it rather than merely state
> \"we\'re different\".
>
> **7. Reduce reputational drag.** Remove ambiguity around parts,
> process, logistics and next steps that would cause a customer to
> hesitate or mistrust the business.
>
> **8. Build memory around a small number of beliefs.** Do not create a
> new slogan for every page.

-   Is it saying something competitors could copy without changing
    anything?

-   Is the claim evidenced?

-   Is the technical word necessary?

-   Is there a clearer sentence?

-   Is the next action obvious?
